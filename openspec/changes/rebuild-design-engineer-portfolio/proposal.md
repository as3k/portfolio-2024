# Change: Rebuild the portfolio around Design Engineering

## Why

The portfolio has credible design, product, and engineering work, but its public positioning is fragmented and several claims are inconsistent or technically overstated. Recruiters do not encounter the strongest evidence first.

## What changes

- Establish **Design Engineer** as Zachary Guerrero’s public identity, with **UX Engineer** as the adjacent role; preserve historical employer-issued titles.
- Align homepage, metadata, schema, navigation language, About, Process, Projects, resume CTA language, and OG images around that positioning.
- Rebuild the Member Splash case study as evidence-led mini-case studies, beginning with Check-In and Splash Cards, using only confirmed facts.
- Rebuild PXC around its terminal interaction model, safe destructive actions, mobile constraint, implementation, and self-use validation.
- Prepare a confidential, anonymized Pacific Life case-study framework without publishing it until facts are reconstructed and approved.
- Establish a meaningful flagship hierarchy: Member Splash, PXC, Pacific Life when ready, then supporting work.
- Remove or qualify unsupported, inconsistent, absolute, stale, and technically misleading claims.
- Address the portfolio technical-quality findings in a separately prioritized pass, including public draft handling, real access control, the tailored-resume API, analytics/privacy alignment, sitemap, accessibility, and image performance.
- Add concise in-page navigation to long flagship case studies, make Enterprise Benefits the third homepage proof point, and retain a small developer-console easter egg.

## Impact

- **Affected surfaces:** homepage, About, Process, Projects, Resume, metadata, JSON-LD, Open Graph images, work MDX, supporting blog claims, technical infrastructure, and the developer-console message.
- **Affected content:** Member Splash, PXC, Manta, Art Healing Hearts, Cydrion, High Rapid Networks, plus the future Pacific Life case study.
- **Breaking changes:** none intended. Existing URLs remain stable unless a future approved decision requires otherwise.
